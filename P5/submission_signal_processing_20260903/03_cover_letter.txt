Dear Editors of Signal Processing,

Please consider our manuscript, "Power-Certified Model-Order Selection with
Abstention for Sparse Multichannel Transient Signals," as a Research Paper.

The paper asks when finite, noisy, grouped transients support a shared spectral
order and when an estimator should abstain because adjacent rates cannot be
resolved. The method combines information improvement, held-unit prediction,
local information, rate separation, cross-pencil stability, and a design-power
certificate. It returns selective evidence about the minimum supported finite
realization rather than a forced full-coverage classification.

A 1,080-trial atlas shows that matrix-pencil BIC selects 60 inaccurate rank-two
recoveries among 157 rank-two selections; the complete gate retains 74 accurate
supports and no inaccurate support under the declared design. On a separate
1,152-trial evaluation, power certification achieves 0.2943 coverage and 0.1386
selective risk with zero observed false order elevation. Five matrix-pencil,
block-Hankel, and shared-Prony criteria quantify full-coverage error tradeoffs.
Public PVA, copper-alloy, gas-sensor, hydraulic, and cable-ageing data provide
support, refusal, transfer, and sensitivity cases. The cable decision is stable
across 12 post-result windows, explicitly not independent replications.

The paper fits Signal Processing through grouped spectral estimation,
model-order selection, finite-resolution analysis, detection with abstention,
and comparisons with established spectral criteria. Claims are conditional on
calibrated observation designs; we do not claim unconditional classifier
superiority or prospective validation.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has
been approved by all authors. It uses public non-human datasets and synthetic
experiments; no human participants or animal experiments are involved. The
authors declare no competing interests.
