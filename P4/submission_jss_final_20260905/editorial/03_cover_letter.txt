Dear Editors of the Journal of Systems and Software,

Please consider our manuscript, "Executable Evidence for Testing
Differentiable Numerical Components: Strategy Increments and External Defect
Cases," as a Research Paper.

Numerical components increasingly cross solver, learning, device, dtype, and
deployment boundaries, yet a successful call or automatic-differentiation
trace does not establish numerical or execution reliability. We present an
executable testing and qualification method that binds operating domain,
independent reference, tolerances, execution policy, provenance, and decision
scope in one machine-readable evidence record.

Four evidence levels detect 3, 5, 8, and 10 of 10 declared fault classes. On
240 PyTorch external-interface trials, detection rises from 25.00% to 100%. A
matched NumPy/SciPy/PyTorch benchmark reaches 100% detection on 252 injected
faults while all 36 clean controls pass. Representation equivalence derived
from SciPy #8906 detects 32/32 variants missed by a weak padded example.
Unchanged runners reproduce official buggy and fixed behavior for PyTorch
#80770 and SciPy #8906/#15620, yielding three complete release-level pairs
across two independently maintained projects and three failure modes.

The work fits JSS as an empirical software-testing study: it compares testing
strategies, evaluates production interfaces and historical defects, and
reports explicit threats to validity. It does not claim universal defect
coverage or field prevalence; seeds and variants are within-family trials.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has
been approved by all authors. The study uses public non-human datasets and
software experiments; no human participants or animal experiments are
involved. The authors declare no competing interests.
