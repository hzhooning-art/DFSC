# P4 Independent Paper Replan

> **Archived route.** This inverse-identification/structural-mismatch plan was
> stopped after the discrepancy-ladder exit test. The active replacement is
> `P4_NEW_ROUTE_PLAN.md`, which studies error-aware active experiment design.

## Working title

**Error-Aware Differentiable Identification of Fractional Operators under
Structural Mismatch**

## Decision

The previous P4 route, parameter-conditioned forward family learning, is not
strong enough as an independent paper. It overlaps with P1's trainable
fractional parameters and P3's hybrid residual composition, and its forcing
extension is not yet numerically certified.

P4 is therefore redefined around a different scientific object:

> reliable inverse identification and selective uncertainty assessment for
> fractional operators when both the numerical forward model and the model
> structure may be imperfect.

This is an independent paper direction, not a larger version of P3.

## Research question

Given sparse/noisy observations `y` generated by an unknown fractional dynamic,
can a differentiable MLSL-based inverse workflow distinguish:

1. uncertainty caused by the numerical value/gradient approximation;
2. uncertainty caused by finite/noisy observations;
3. structural mismatch caused by using the wrong fractional family;

and produce parameter intervals together with an explicit accept/abstain
decision?

## Proposed method: EAFI

The proposed P4 object is an **Error-Aware Fractional Identification** workflow
with four coupled components:

### 1. Forward-error-aware objective

Use the P2 value and gradient diagnostics to augment the inverse objective with
a numerical-error floor. A small residual is not treated as meaningful when it
is below the certified forward/gradient uncertainty.

### 2. Gradient-consistent profile inference

Construct profile likelihood or profile loss intervals for `alpha`, `beta`, and
operator coefficients, while re-optimizing nuisance parameters. The interval
threshold is inflated by the measured numerical-gradient error instead of being
chosen independently of the solver.

### 3. Structural mismatch score

Combine multi-horizon residual shape, parameter-perturbation response, and
profile curvature into a mismatch statistic. This distinguishes a broad,
flat-but-consistent parameter region from a numerically unstable optimizer.

### 4. Selective inference contract

Return either:

- accepted parameter intervals with diagnostics, or
- abstention with a reason such as insufficient identifiability, unresolved
  numerical error, or structural mismatch.

The output is not only a fitted parameter vector.

## Why this is independent from P1--P3

| Work | Primary output | What P4 adds |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Differentiable forward primitive | not an inverse uncertainty method |
| P2 | Value/gradient numerical reliability | not structural identifiability or inference coverage |
| P3 | Selective forward hybrid routing | not parameter intervals under mismatch |
| P4 | Error-aware inverse inference contract | joint numerical uncertainty, identifiability, mismatch, and abstention |

The novelty claim must be the inference contract and its calibrated evidence,
not the use of MLSL alone.

## Minimum algorithmic baselines

P4 must compare against:

- ordinary differentiable least-squares without numerical-error inflation;
- classical L1/Prony or stretched-exponential fitting where applicable;
- profile inference without gradient-error correction;
- a neural inverse baseline without the structured forward model;
- an oracle setting with exact synthetic forward values.

## Experimental program

### A. Known-truth identifiability

Use controlled fractional systems with known `alpha`, `beta`, and spectral
parameters. Vary observation density, noise level, time horizon, and parameter
correlation. Report interval coverage, width, bias, and optimizer failure rate.

### B. Numerical-error injection

Deliberately vary evaluator terms, Krylov dimension, dtype, and adaptive
budget. Test whether EAFI widens or rejects intervals when numerical error
increases, while ordinary inference remains overconfident.

### C. Structural mismatch

Fit the wrong family: fractional versus stretched exponential, one-order versus
two-order, and incorrect spectral-rate parameterization. Report harmful
acceptance rate and abstention precision.

### D. Independent public data

Use at least two public trajectory or relaxation datasets that were not used to
design the decision thresholds. Compare parameter coverage and model-selection
behavior with conventional baselines.

### E. Multi-seed and cluster statistics

Use at least five seeds, complete-trajectory or specimen-level resampling, and
report mean/std plus clustered confidence intervals. Point estimates alone do
not satisfy the P4 evidence requirement.

## Promotion gates for an independent paper

P4 is allowed to proceed as an independent manuscript only if all conditions
hold:

1. nominal 90% or 95% interval coverage is demonstrated on held-out known-truth
   cases;
2. coverage remains calibrated after controlled numerical-error injection;
3. structural mismatch increases abstention or harmful-inference reduction
   relative to ordinary fitting;
4. the method is not worse than the strongest conventional baseline on all
   public datasets;
5. the result survives five seeds and cluster-aware resampling;
6. the forced primitive is not required for the main claim unless P2 certifies
   its operating regime first.

If any of gates 1--3 fails, P4 returns to a DFSC/P2 extension. If only gate 4
fails on a public domain, the paper must report a limitation rather than claim
universal superiority.

## Expected contribution level

If the gates pass, P4 can be an independent SciML/numerical-inference paper
because it contributes a new inference contract and calibrated failure policy,
not merely another neural architecture. If the gates fail, the work should
remain a supporting module and not be forced into a separate publication.
