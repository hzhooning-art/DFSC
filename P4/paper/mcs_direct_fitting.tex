\section{Direct fitting followed by target prediction}
\label{sec:direct-fits}
We supplement the enclosure controls with three explicit point-prediction baselines on the same 256 calibrated cases. All methods receive the same 80 response observations, exact loading histories, nonnegative unit-mass model and upper rate cap $4$. The generating rates and target truth are not provided to fitting or model selection. The existing results were already inspected when this comparison was designed; it is a declared-before-fitting supplemental analysis, not an independent blind validation or new random cohort.

The first baseline fits nonnegative weights summing to one on 64 logarithmic positive rates from $10^{-8}$ to $4$, plus an equilibrium atom. The second adds an $L^2$ penalty on transient weights, excluding equilibrium, selected from $\{0,10^{-7},10^{-5},10^{-3},10^{-1}\}$. This penalty is specific to the finite representation, not a continuum spectral prior. The third fits free-rate Prony models with two, four or eight transient modes and an equilibrium atom. Its rates range over $[0,4]$, without the earlier two-mode routine's restrictive positive-rate floor. Nonnegative normalized weights use a stick-breaking parameterization. Three deterministic initializations per order span slow and faster rates; constrained least-squares iterations are limited to 350 function evaluations per start. These are transparent local implementations using SciPy \cite{scipy}, not reproductions or new variants of a named published fitting algorithm.

For regularization and order selection, the trailing quarter of each of the three loading records is withheld: 4 pilot and 8 observations from each confirmation record, totalling 20. Selection uses only these response residuals, followed by refitting all 80 observations. The deterministic within-record split is not a material-level external validation. Candidate-order failures, failed starts and final-refit failures are retained. A successful nonlinear termination is not a global-optimum certificate; failures under the stated budget do not prove that Prony prediction is inherently unsuccessful.

Table~\ref{tab:direct-fits} reports target absolute errors and separate failure counts through its success denominators. The point-error thresholds $0.10$ for $G(20)$ and $0.05$ for held-out stress are one-half of the existing diameter thresholds. They do not replace the prospective pair-diameter rule or its original decisions. Symmetric and classical continuous-interval midpoints are included as predeclared point estimates, without claiming mean-square optimality. An interval and a point estimate are not interchangeable competitors: an accurate fitted point does not certify the entire compatible class, while a valid outer interval need not minimize average prediction error. No calibrated statistical prediction interval is supplied for the fitting baselines.

Both grid-based fits terminate successfully in all 512 case/window records, with no joint point-tolerance exceedance. Free-rate Prony succeeds in 194 short-window and 204 long-window fits; 114 final fits fail to produce a successful termination, and 465 candidate-order fits also fail. There is one joint target-tolerance exceedance among successful short-window Prony fits and none at the longer window. No band-compatible successful fitting baseline exceeds these target tolerances in this dataset. Thus these records do not empirically demonstrate a good-fit/bad-target failure or broad superiority of interval midpoints. At window 8 the grid least-squares estimate has smaller mean absolute errors than either midpoint; at window 16 the midpoint errors are smaller. These are descriptive paired comparisons, not a population superiority test. Under-converged nonlinear fits require a stronger numerical investigation before an adverse Prony ranking could be justified.

All 12 calibration-underestimation cases remain in the unconditional results. The full supplementary records additionally report each spectral family and the calibration-valid and invalid subsets, response residuals, band compatibility, target errors, selected orders and penalties, and convergence statuses. Offline calibration validity does not enter fitting. The two windows share noise realizations within a case, and two targets share fitted parameters; there are 256 cases, not 1024 independent trials. Computation used four worker processes; selection and refit costs are recorded, but historical LP times and this parallel wall time are not promoted as an equal-resource speed comparison.
